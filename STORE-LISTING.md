## Long description — English

Most to-do apps ask you to sort and remember. The One Thing does that part: it
looks at what is late, who owes you a reply, and who you have not spoken to in
a while, and tells you what to do next.

• NEXT: a short, ranked list on Home. Late tasks first, then replies you are owed, then today, then people going quiet. Each card says why it is there and puts the real action one tap away.
• TALK TO ADD: say "Call Sara tomorrow at 10", "I told Omar I'd send the quote Friday" or "Waiting on Layla for the contract". It understands promises and waiting too, and asks only what it needs. English, Gulf and Egyptian Arabic.
• A PAGE FOR EVERY PERSON: open a name and see your history with them like a thread. Write the last comment, see what is still open, and call, WhatsApp, email or schedule a meeting from the same place.
• WRITTEN FOR YOU: drafts that read like a senior business writer. Dear {name}, a clear subject, a proper close. Chat messages stay short. Nothing is ever sent for you; you press send.
• SEND TO MANY, ONE BY ONE: pick a label or a group, and the next person is ready each time you come back. Fifty messages in a day stays quick.
• BRING YOUR PEOPLE: import from Excel, CSV, PDF or a vCard file, scan a business card, or pick from your phone. Repeats are caught before they are added.
• KEEP IN TOUCH: set how often you want to hear from someone, add labels like Client or Lead, and add a second number or email.
• Dark mode, 12-hour clock, and Arabic right to left.

Your tasks and contacts stay on your phone unless you sign in to sync.

## Privacy answers (Apple "App Privacy" and Play "Data safety")

Collected, linked to the account, not used for tracking:
- Contact info: name, email (account); the name, number, email, address, labels and notes of people the user saves (synced only when signed in).
- User content: tasks, reminders, notes and history the person writes.
- Identifiers: account ID.
- Usage data: product-analytics events listed in `DATA_MODEL.md`; no advertising SDKs.
- Purchases: subscription status.

Not collected: location, health, browsing history, financial info, advertising ID.
Photos: a picture the person picks for a contact or their own profile is copied into the app and stays on the phone; it is never uploaded.
Camera: used to read a business card on the device and to take a contact's picture. The photo of a card is not kept; the text read from it goes into a contact the person confirms.
Microphone and speech recognition: used on the device's own recogniser only while recording a note; audio is not stored by the app.
Contacts: only a contact the person picks is read; the address book is never read on its own.
Files: a spreadsheet, PDF or contacts file the person chooses is read on the device to import contacts; the file itself is not uploaded.
Deletion: in Settings, and at `onlyonething.app/delete`.
Apple Privacy Manifest note: `NSPhotoLibraryUsageDescription` and `NSCameraUsageDescription` are now real uses (contact photos, card scan).
